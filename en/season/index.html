Season & Competitions

Season

The season startson July 1.

The figure skating season runs from July 1 to June 30 of the following year. Competitions start with junior events in August and continue until April of the next year.

Grand Prix

A Final spot is earnedover 2 Grand Prix events.

The 2026-27 Grand Prix Series runs from late October to late November, with one event every weekend in 6 countries.

A skater can enter up to 2 of the 6 events.

Each event awards points by placement. A skater adds up the points from 2 events, and the top 6 skaters in each discipline go on to the Grand Prix Final.

The Final is held in Chongqing (Dec 10–13). Only the 6 skaters in each discipline who advanced from the series compete.

Fall broadcasts talk about standings and points because skaters are competing for the 6 places in the Final.

Calendar

Competitions run from August to April of the next year.

The highlight of the 2026-27 season is the World Championships in March. Before that, the Grand Prix Final and the Japan Championships take place in December, and the continental championships in January and February.

Highlights

World Championships results set next season’s entry spots

A country can send up to 3 skaters to the World Championships. That number is set by adding up the placements of the country’s top two skaters at the previous season’s World Championships. The higher they place, the more entry spots the country gets for the next season.

Highlights

Olympic entry spots also come from the World Championships

The number of skaters a country can send to the Olympic Winter Games is also decided mainly by the results of the World Championships in the season before the Olympics. This is why broadcasts talk about “spots”.

Highlights

An exhibition is a show of performances unlike those in competition

An exhibition, or gala, is a show held at the end of a competition, featuring the top finishers and other skaters. Unlike a competition, it has no set elements, scores or rankings, and the skaters choose the music, costumes, props and staging freely. With nothing to win or lose, skaters focus on entertaining the audience, with playful programs and kinds of expression you do not see in competition.

Off season

Ice shows are held in the off season.

They focus on entertainment on ice and are not bound by winners and losers or the strict rules of a competition. Olympic medalists, top skaters from the World Championships and professional skaters, among others, perform and express themselves freely. You can enjoy new programs, group numbers with many skaters and performances with other artists, which you do not see in competition.
